# PFAD

**A study of time, space, and the paths not taken.**

Modern route-finding is impossibly fast. PFAD makes its underlying computation
experienceable at human scale: a slow replay of genuine algorithm events over
a real geographic network. Its first subject is Switzerland.

Roads emerge from darkness as the algorithm encounters them. Possibilities
spread across the landscape; valleys channel the search and junctions create
new possibilities. The final route remains visible within the larger field of
roads considered. The answer is almost incidental. The subject is the search.

Every displayed exploration must correspond to a recorded event. A road merely
absent from the final route is not necessarily rejected by the algorithm. Event
order and wall-clock execution timing are distinct; the interface must identify
which clock is being replayed. Whole journeys are not exhaustively enumerated.

Begin visually with Gleislicht’s luminous geography. Grid84 may inform framing,
typography or computational readouts, but this remains an artistic decision.
Let a real trace reveal what visual treatment it can carry. Avoid fabricated
networks or a progress animation presented as genuine computation.

The initial proof concerns a country-scale Zürich–Geneva search. The edition
remains unnumbered. Measured computation time must replace the provisional
“twenty milliseconds” in any factual presentation.
